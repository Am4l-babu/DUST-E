The bin gets a face, a voice and two hands

Two SSD1306 OLED eyes, a MAX98357A speaker and two servo arms, added to the ESP32 + L298N web-remote build. Every part is optional, and the safety layer still has the last word. Below that: the upgrades worth doing next, each linked to where the idea came from.

What was added

Four new modules under firmware/TrashBotWeb/src/. They hook into the event stream the sound bank already has, so anything that could play a sound can now also move the face and the arms, even when no clip is assigned to that event.

Eyes

src/face/eyes.h · eyes.cpp
  • 14 expressions, drawn from numbers (no bitmaps) and eased every frame
  • Auto-blink, idle glancing, gaze follows steering and people
  • Runs in its own task on core 0 at 25 fps, so the I2C bus never slows the motors
  • Both OLEDs are checked at boot. The HW tab shows ONLINE or MISSING

Speaker

src/sound/speaker.h · speaker.cpp
  • Plays WAV, MP3 and ADTS AAC from LittleFS through I2S (ESP8266Audio 1.9.9)
  • Reads the WAV format tag. Your hi_chellam_i_love_u.wav is really MP3 inside a WAV file, and it is decoded as MP3
  • The phone shows “THE BIN SAYS …” instead of playing an echo
  • E-stop sound cuts off whatever is playing

Hands

src/hands/hands.h · hands.cpp
  • 12 gestures: wave, shrug, celebrate, angry, flail, refuse, poke, bow, facepalm, point, sleep, rest
  • Smoothstep keyframes, clamped to angle limits and a 400°/s slew ceiling
  • Servo pulses turn off at rest, so the arms don't hum or jitter
  • E-stop: the gesture stops, arms park at 60°/s, then go limp

Body + dashboard

src/body/ · webLayer.cpp · data/app.js
  • One table maps all 31 events to an expression and a gesture
  • Mood sets the resting face. NORMAL MODE cancelled: the eyes look at the button, then the arm pokes it
  • New WebSocket messages {"type":"gesture"} and {"type":"face"}
  • A BODY card on the HW tab has test buttons and live servo angles

Eye expressions

Each expression drawn at 128×64 per panel, using the same shape table and drawing steps as the firmware: rounded rectangle, flat lid, slanted lid, happy cut. Left panel on the left. confused is the only asymmetric one: the right eye shrinks to 55%.

Wiring diagram

I²C (eyes) I²S (speaker) servo signal 5 V servo/audio rail
Two eyes, one addressMost SSD1306 modules ship at 0x3C. Move the resistor on the back of one from 0x78 to 0x7A to make it 0x3D. Or set EYES_SPLIT_BUS and wire the right eye to GPIO 5/15 instead.
Servo power is the first bugA servo can pull about 1.5 A at startup. Powered from the ESP32's USB 5 V, it browns out and resets the board. Use a separate rail with a common ground.
Pins to leave aloneGPIO 12 sets the flash voltage at boot, 6–11 are the flash chip, and 34–39 are input-only. The new map avoids all of them.

Web research

Projects it borrows from

FluxGarage RoboEyes

Eyes drawn from parameters on Adafruit GFX, with moods like happy, tired and angry. The shape vocabulary here follows it, extended to one full panel per eye.github.com/FluxGarage/RoboEyes

playfultechnology esp32-eyes

Cozmo-inspired eyes drawn each frame from a set of parameters instead of bitmaps. Same approach as here.github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes

Desktop WALL-E (Hackster)

ESP32-S3 robot with servos for head and arms plus an OLED, gesturing while it talks. The model for hands that react to speech.hackster.io

Useless Box with ESP32 + MG90S

A servo finger that switches itself off. The poke gesture copies its timing on the web build, which has no real finger.thingiverse.com/thing:4728848

MAX98357A + ESP32 guides

Wiring, decoupling caps, and playing WAV from LittleFS.makerguides.com DFRobot wiki

ESP8266Audio

The MP3, AAC and WAV decoders plus I2S output that the speaker module uses.github.com/earlephilhower/ESP8266Audio
